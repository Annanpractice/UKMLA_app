# ScotGEM prescribing 2026–27 — UKMLA pharmacology-card audit
Audit date: 23 October 2026. Source: `ScotGEM_Prescribing_Learning_Points_2026-27.pdf` (15-page consolidation of 146 Mentimeter slides).

## Scope
Compared all 19 taught cases against 142 existing cards in `v2/pharmacology-data-*.js`. Original case answers are not necessarily complete clinical protocols. Verified high-risk distinctions using current MHRA, RCUK, eMC, NHS SPS, FSRH and Scottish Palliative Care sources. The audit adds **8 targeted cards**, refines **4 existing cards**, and leaves well-covered content in place (150 cards after changes).

## Important corrections/caveats
1. **ACS:** severe suspected ischaemic pain can require 300 mg aspirin and emergency transfer even with a normal first ECG; GTN is only symptomatic treatment.
2. **Hyperkalaemia:** do not equate the deck's insulin/glucose selection with first action in ECG-toxic hyperkalaemia; immediate IV calcium stabilises cardiac membranes.
3. **Sulfasalazine:** NHS SPS (updated 7 Jan 2026) gives every 2 weeks until stable for 6 weeks, then monthly 3 months, with ongoing every 12 weeks for first year. Updated BSR guideline may require local protocol verification.
4. **Midazolam:** Epistatus 2.5 mg = 0.25 mL; Buccolam 2.5 mg = 0.5 mL. Never transfer a volume unchanged between brands.
5. **Fentanyl:** conversion ratios are approximations for established opioid use, requiring double check; fever and applied heat increase exposure.
6. **Rivaroxaban:** 15 and 20 mg doses require food; when crushed, follow immediately with food. DOACs do not require warfarin-like INR checks.
7. **Adrenaline:** 10-year-old standard IM 300 micrograms (0.3 mL of 1 mg/mL). Never confuse the IM solution and route with IV resuscitation practice.
8. **Combined contraception:** annual BP and BMI are part of a full medical-eligibility review rather than stand-alone monitoring.
9. **Levothyroxine:** calcium carbonate can reduce absorption; separate by 4 h before escalating dose.
